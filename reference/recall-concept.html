What I noticed

Saving a place costs one tap. Organizing it costs a decision, at the worst possible moment.

Under-capture"I'll remember this place. I never do."The moment passes and nothing is saved.
Over-capture"I saved it. No idea which one it was."Saved, then lost among hundreds of unlabeled pins.

Three flows

Inside Google Maps' existing shell. Only the new parts are new.

1 · Capture

Tap Save. That's the whole job.

Tap a place→Save→3 AI tags
Key decisionSaving and tagging are separate from adding a note or photo. The three-second path is a complete end state.
2 · Browse and filter

Filter by any tag, AI or your own.

Saved tab→Pick a tag→Grid narrows live
Key decisionFilters are built only from what you saved, so they never return empty.
3 · Ask

What you know comes before what's new.

Ask a question→Saved first→Discovery if needed
Key decisionEvery answer checks your saved places first, and says which results are yours and which are new.

Scope decision

A business product I chose not to design.

Selling "why customers save you" insights to local businesses.

Proven
  • Restaurants pay for occasion-level guest insight. SevenRooms is built on it.
Not proven
  • Would private saves ever be shared, even anonymized?
  • Would a small business get enough saves to mean anything?
  • Would owners trust strangers' habits over their own data?

"I chose not to design screens for a hypothesis I hadn't validated yet." Next step: 3 to 5 interviews with business owners.
